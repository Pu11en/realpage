## Patterns To Borrow

- **Headlines work best when they name an operator anxiety.** Examples: AI leads managing the team, CRM lying, acquisition due diligence surprises, technology not automatically profitable.
- **Questions outperform declarations for discussion-style posts.** Recent Discussions titles are often peer-benchmark prompts: "What are you using?", "How are people handling?", "Does your company still...?"
- **Strong blog posts are usually practical and mid-length.** The highest-hit blog examples sampled were mostly **479-784 words**, with one long story-led exception.
- **The hook usually starts from a familiar workflow, then reframes it.** AI replies faster, but who controls the handoff? Tech creates efficiency, but what metric proves it? Acquisition closes, but what happens in the first 30 days?
- **MFI readers seem receptive to vendor/tech content when the post is operator-first.** Posts with vendor-adjacent categories can perform, but they frame around staffing pressure, ROI, conversion, implementation speed, due diligence, or resident/prospect outcomes.
- **Visible engagement is more passive than conversational.** Many high-hit blogs show few or no comments. A CraneSignal post should optimize for usefulness and clicks, not expect a large comment thread.

## What A CraneSignal Post Should Copy

- **Use a discussion-native headline.** Best first option: "When a property sells, how do you know if the software stack is about to change?"
- **Open with a real operator/seller moment.** Example angle: a property changes hands, the website still shows the old manager, permits show work starting, and every vendor is guessing when to reach out.
- **Frame CraneSignal's world as a decision problem, not a data product.** "Which buildings are worth calling this week?" is stronger than "we detect property-management software."
- **Use concrete switch signals.** Recent sale, new owner/manager, construction or renovation permits, public-record changes, and visible software/vendor traces.
- **Keep the length around 600-800 words for a blog.** For a discussion post, keep it under 250 words and end with one precise question.
- **Ask for peer practice at the end.** Good CTA: "For those selling into owner/operators, what signal has been most reliable that a property is actually open to switching vendors?"
- **Sound like an operator peer, not a startup pitch.** The post should be useful even if the reader never clicks CraneSignal.

## What To Avoid

- **Avoid a product-launch announcement.** MFI's stronger examples lead with the reader's pain, not the vendor's feature list.
- **Avoid generic AI/proptech claims.** The category is crowded; "AI improves operations" is less compelling than "your CRM may be missing the trigger that makes a lead worth calling."
- **Avoid over-indexing on public records as a surveillance story.** The public-record example is useful, but CraneSignal should keep the tone practical and source-backed.
- **Avoid too many signals in one post.** Pick one scenario, such as recent acquisition, then mention other signals as supporting checks.
- **Avoid a hard demo CTA on the first MFI post.** A better first CTA is a question or a practical checklist; link softly only if allowed and natural.

## Recommended Post Shape

### Blog version

Working title: **When a Property Sells, How Do You Know If the Software Stack Is About to Change?**

Outline:

1. Open with the common miss: vendors learn about a property sale after the new team has already made vendor decisions.
2. Explain why recent sale/new owner is a useful signal but not enough by itself.
3. Add the practical checks: manager change, website/vendor traces, permits/construction, leasing status, public records, and source links.
4. Show the sales-ops implication: prioritize accounts by likelihood of change, not just unit count or market.
5. End with the peer question: "What has been your most reliable sign that an owner/operator is actually open to switching systems?"

### Discussion version

Draft prompt:

> When a property changes hands, what signals tell you the new owner is actually open to changing software or vendors?
>
> Recent sale alone feels too broad. I would look for a manager change, active construction or permit activity, stale/changed website tech, and public records that suggest a repositioning is underway.
>
> For people selling into owner/operators: what signal has been most reliable for timing outreach before the vendor decisions are already made?

Best bet: start with the discussion version, because MFI's discussion area rewards practical peer questions and this keeps CraneSignal from sounding promotional.
