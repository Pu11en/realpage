## Final Copy (ready, on the board)

**Post title:** When a property changes hands, what changes first?

**Post body:**

When a property changes hands, the new owner almost never keeps everything as is. In the first few months something gets replaced: the management company, the leasing process, the accounting system, a maintenance vendor, or the software.

For anyone selling into apartments, the hard part is timing. Reach out too early and you are noise. Reach out too late and the decisions are already made.

So I have been testing public signals for which properties are in motion:

1. A management company change
2. Active permits or renovation work
3. A new or rebranded leasing website
4. Public records that show a repositioning
5. Old vendor traces disappearing from the property site

A recent sale by itself tells you less than any of these. Plenty of owners change nothing for a year after closing.

For the people here who work inside property management or sell to owner operators: which of these has been the most reliable for you?

**Closing line (in post or first comment):**

What would you add or remove from this list?
