## Multifamily Insiders

### Signals after a sale discussion (blog or discussion)

Title: When a property changes hands, what changes first?

Body:

When a property changes hands, the new owner almost never keeps everything as is. In the first few months something gets replaced: the management company, the leasing process, the accounting system, a maintenance vendor, or the software.

For anyone selling into apartments, the hard part is timing. Reach out too early and you are noise. Reach out too late and the decisions are already made.

So I have been testing public signals for which properties are in motion:

1. A management company change
2. Active permits or renovation work
3. A new or rebranded leasing website
4. Public records that show a repositioning
5. Old vendor traces disappearing from the property site

A recent sale by itself tells you less than any of these. Plenty of owners change nothing for a year after closing.

For the people here who work inside property management or sell to owner operators: which of these has been the most reliable for you?

Closing line: What would you add or remove from this list?

Soft context: I am building CraneSignal around this question. Pressure testing the signals here before they turn into a pitch.

### r/PptyMgmtSoftware feedback post (text only, before the video version)

Title: I detect which PMS a building runs, plus public switch signals. Fellow builders, what would make this useful vs misleading?

Body:

I sell to the people who sell to apartments: maintenance software, inspection tools, resident apps. The complaint I hear from their sales teams is always the same shape. Their prospect list is every apartment building in the state, so reps spend the week on accounts that were never in motion.

So I am building the timing layer instead. From public sources only:

Which property-management or resident-facing software a property runs
Switch signals: recent sale, new owner or management company, permits, construction, lease-ups

The Texas tracker holds 1,876 buildings right now and only a few hundred have a real signal on them. Those are the ones I hand over.

Questions for people who actually build in this space:

1. Would your sales team trust detected PMS from public traces? What earns that trust: a source link per claim, a confidence score, both?
2. Where does this get misleading or unfair to the properties?

I would rather be told it is a bad idea here than find out from a churned customer.

Timing is the part nobody sells. A building that changed hands last month behaves nothing like one that has been run by the same team for years, even though the unit count is identical. Different budget, different urgency, different conversation.
