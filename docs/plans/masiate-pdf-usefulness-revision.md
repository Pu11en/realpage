## How The Revised PDF Will Sort The Findings

Replace the headline "Top Properties" with **"Masiate Opportunity Review: What Is Known And What Still Needs Checking"**.

Each reviewed project gets one clear status, grounded in dated evidence:

- **Owner or tenant requesting a contractor:** a current, project-specific request for estimates or contractor bids is in the saved evidence; show scope, deadline, source and buying party. This section may be empty.
- **Work fits, but hiring is unknown:** the saved evidence describes relevant work, but does not establish whether a contractor has been selected. These are verification candidates, not sales-ready leads.
- **Contractor already named for this scope:** show the company, role, exact job and source; remove it from the owner-hiring shortlist. A sign contractor does not establish the building contractor, and one suite's builder does not establish another suite's builder.
- **Hold or exclude:** weak service fit, expired tender, unrelated location, unresolved identity, old/completed work, or evidence too thin for a useful next step; explain which reason applies.

Do not present a named contractor as proof that every trade is filled, or turn
it into a subcontract lead without evidence of an unmet subcontract need.
Do not describe a project as contractor-free merely because the record omits
the contractor. Accessibility review, a construction permit and a contract
award are different facts. "Issued for bidding" on drawings is a research
clue, not proof that the owner is still soliciting bids today.

### What Each Useful Profile Must Say

- **What work is actually described?** Separate the source wording from our inference about Masiate's fit.
- **What could Masiate do?** Name the relevant service; do not assume its crew size, bonding, commercial qualifications or capacity from its website.
- **Who would be buying that work?** Owner, tenant or main contractor, with the role established or explicitly unknown.
- **Is someone already named for that exact work?** Give the contractor evidence, or say unknown.
- **What makes this worth checking?** A concrete reason, not just a large project budget or recent registration.
- **What is the business contact route?** Identify the source and whether it reaches a buyer, designer, contractor or general office; no personal homeowner contact details.
- **What single missing fact determines the next step?** For example, whether the tenant still needs a main contractor, rather than a generic instruction to research more.
- **How old is the evidence?** Show both the source date and check date, distinguish estimated from actual construction dates, and disclose reporting gaps.
