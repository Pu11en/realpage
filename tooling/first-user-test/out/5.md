# Persona 5

You are Dana, a VP of sales at an apartment internet provider; your market is Tulsa, Oklahoma. Your goal: find leads for Tulsa, and if the site doesn't cover it, find out what you can do about that.

## Pages visited
https://cranesignal.com
https://cranesignal.com/
https://cranesignal.com/#monday
https://app.cranesignal.com/index.html
https://app.cranesignal.com/map.html

## Report
GOAL REACHED: partly

STEPS:
1. Landed on cranesignal.com. Read the hero: a lead-gen product that surfaces new apartment buildings early (council approval → permit → construction → lease up). Sample lead shown was Richardson, TX.
2. Scrolled the homepage. Saw value props, sample leads, and CTAs: "Start free", "Open CraneSignal", "Book a 15 minute call", plus "Don't see your area? Ask and we'll add it" and a "Free Lead Pack: download every lead in your area as a PDF".
3. Clicked "The list" — it only jumped to an on-page anchor (#monday) showing quotes and sample Austin, TX data, not a coverage list.
4. Scrolled down and found the instruction to "Pick your state and region" and the CTA "Open CraneSignal".
5. Clicked "Open CraneSignal" — it opened the app in a new tab (app.cranesignal.com).
6. In the app, clicked "Map". Saw a US map shading "Apartment buildings by software" across "the 15 states building the most apartments", with amber markers = the states they have leads in (clickable to open a table). Oklahoma was not listed/amber among the 15 lead states.
7. A chat panel "Ask CraneSignal" popped up but requires a free account, so I closed it (did not sign up).
8. Found the "Don't see your area?" form — its placeholder example was literally "City or county, state (e.g. Tulsa, OK)".
9. Entered "Tulsa, OK" and clicked "Ask for it".
10. Confirmation appeared: "Got it. We'll add Tulsa, OK." — so Tulsa is not currently covered and a request to add it was registered.

CONFUSING:
- "The list" in the nav — I expected a coverage/market list (states or metros covered), but it just scrolled to an on-page section (#monday) with quotes and Austin data.
- "Open CraneSignal" vs "Start free" vs "Book a 15 minute call" — unclear which one gets you into the actual data; I guessed "Open CraneSignal".
- The map legend "15 states building the most apartments" vs "Amber markers are the states we have leads in" — two different sets, so I was unsure whether a shaded-but-not-amber state (like OK) counts as covered.
- "View as" dropdown with values Everyone / RealPage / Yardi / Entrata — I didn't understand what it did.
- The chat panel requiring a "free account" when the homepage marketed "no sign-in needed" for the free early access.

MISSING:
- No explicit coverage list / market selector by state or metro where I could simply type "Tulsa" and see covered/not covered.
- No clear indication of what happens after "Ask for it" (no timeline, no confirmation email field was required, unclear if Tulsa would actually be added).
- No pricing or plan detail — just "Start free", "Open CraneSignal", "Book a 15 minute call".
- No Oklahoma/Tulsa lead data anywhere on the site.

FIRST 5 SECONDS:
I thought CraneSignal was a lead-generation tool that finds new apartment buildings early in their construction lifecycle (council approval → permit → construction → lease up), aimed at internet/utility/service providers who want to reach buildings before they open.

WOULD SIGN UP: No. The homepage emphasizes free, no-sign-in access, and there was no clear value in creating an account just to see a demo map that was largely locked behind a chat sign-in. If I were seriously pursuing this market, the missing coverage list and lack of any Tulsa data would have stopped me anyway.

SCORE: 4/10 — the site is easy to scroll and the pitch is clear, but the key question ("is Tulsa covered?") had no direct answer; the nav "The list" didn't list coverage, and I had to hunt for the "Don't see your area?" form to get a yes/no. The map's two-tier legend (shaded states vs amber lead states) added to the confusion.
