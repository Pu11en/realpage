# Persona 5

You are Dana, a VP of sales at an apartment internet provider; your market is Tulsa, Oklahoma. Your goal: find leads for Tulsa, and if the site doesn't cover it, find out what you can do about that.

## Pages visited
https://cranesignal.com
https://cranesignal.com/
https://app.cranesignal.com/index.html
https://app.cranesignal.com/map.html#request-area

## Report
GOAL REACHED: partly

STEPS:
1. Landed on https://cranesignal.com. Saw a headline about getting alerted to new apartment buildings "before anyone else" and a sample lead card: "Legacy Arapaho, Richardson, TX." It said free during early access, "No card." Also saw "Built by Drew Pullen."
2. Scrolled down the whole homepage. It's a lead-alert service for new apartment buildings (new builds, opening soon, recently sold). Sample lead list shown was for the Austin, TX metro (Rainey Street Flats, Parmer Crossing, South Lamar Yards — "3 of 14"). Data sources listed: city records, government housing data, news. Target buyers listed: property management sales, software sales, smart locks/internet, contractors, lease-up agencies.
3. Found the coverage section: "Covered: Texas, Arizona" and "The states we cover so far." Oklahoma was not listed anywhere and Tulsa was never mentioned.
4. Clicked "See the leads free" ("→") which opened a new tab: app.cranesignal.com/map.html (the leads browser / US map).
5. On the map page, the only selectable states were Texas and Arizona; all listed regions were in Texas. It said "Click a highlighted state to see its leads. Grey states aren't covered yet." Oklahoma's state was greyed out / not covered. Verdict: Tulsa is NOT covered.
6. Clicked the "Ask for it" link ("Don't see your area? Tell us the city or county and we'll add its buildings.").
7. Landed on the request-area form. The "City or county, state" field placeholder literally read "Tulsa, OK."
8. Typed "Tulsa, OK" into the field and clicked "Ask for it."
9. Got confirmation on-page: "Got it. We'll add Tulsa, OK." — so the area request was submitted. That is the concrete "what you can do about it" path for an uncovered market.

CONFUSING:
- "See the leads free" and "Browse every lead free" — I wasn't sure if that meant I could see everything or only a preview (it was a preview, "3 of 14").
- "Free during early access" / "No card" — I was unsure whether a free account was still required to see anything useful.
- "Grey states aren't covered yet" — the map is shaded per state but I couldn't tell Oklahoma was grey vs. just unlabeled until I read the legend, so I hesitated over whether Oklahoma was covered.
- "ask for yours" / "Ask for it" — two nearly identical buttons/labels; I wasn't sure at first if they did the same thing.
- "apartment buildings with detected software per state" (the map's aria description) — the word "software" here was ambiguous; I wasn't sure what it meant at the state level.
- The confirmation "Got it. We'll add Tulsa, OK." appeared but with no email/ETA, so I was unsure if it was a real request or just an echo of what I typed.

MISSING:
- Any mention of Tulsa or Oklahoma anywhere on the site (it was absent until the example placeholder).
- Pricing or plan details — the whole site just says "free during early access, no card," so I couldn't tell what it costs later.
- A coverage list by state on the homepage — coverage was only discoverable via the map tab.
- Any timeline/SLA for the "we'll add it" request.
- An obvious "explore Tulsa anyway" fallback — no non-covered-state browsing option.

FIRST 5 SECONDS: I thought it was a real-estate/lead-gen tool that alerts you the moment new apartment buildings are announced, so salespeople can call before competitors. I assumed it would be a national service.

WOULD SIGN UP: No. Two reasons: (1) I was explicitly told not to sign up, and (2) Tulsa isn't covered, so a free account (which only unlocks "who to call" and the call list) wouldn't give me Tulsa leads anyway. I did understand WHY an account exists — it unlocks the "who to call" contact details and call list; the site was clear and honest about that, and the early-access offer ("no card") was low-friction. If Tulsa were covered, I would likely have created the free account since no card is required.

SCORE: 7/10. The site is clean, fast, honest, and I found coverage + the request path quickly. It lost points because coverage wasn't obvious on the homepage, the map took a moment to read, the two "ask" labels were redundant, and the request confirmation gave no expectation of what happens next.
