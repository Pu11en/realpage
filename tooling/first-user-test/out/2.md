You are Kevin, a sales manager at a smart-lock / resident-tech company, skeptical of new tools. Your goal: figure out in under 2 minutes what this site gives you, whether the data is real (sources?), and whether it's free.

## Report
GOAL REACHED: yes

STEPS:
1. Landed on https://cranesignal.com. Hero + a sample lead table (Austin, TX) showing building, units, stage, software, and a 'why call now' reason. Saw an early-access banner and a 'See the leads free' CTA.
2. Scrolled down: found more explanation — the tool surfaces 'early-stage construction signals' for companies selling to apartment buildings (e.g., smart locks / resident tech). A note said it was built by one person (Drew Pullen), free during early access, no credit card.
3. Scrolled further: saw pricing/freeness framing — browsing is free, and a free account (~20 sec, email or Google, no card) unlocks 'who to call', an agent, and a call-list PDF/CSV. Data sources were labeled 'City public records', 'Government housing data', and 'NEWS'.
4. Clicked 'See the leads free →'. It opened app.cranesignal.com in a new tab: a browsable leads catalog (592 Texas leads, dated Sep 15 2026), no account required to look.
5. On a lead, clicked 'See who to call →'. A sign-in panel appeared — the manager name and direct phone are gated behind a free account. I did NOT sign up.
6. Closed the chat/sign-in panel with the × button.
7. Clicked a per-lead 'State project record' link. It opened a new tab to the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR) Architectural Barriers page — a real government record.
8. Verified the source: the CraneSignal lead for 'Groves Apartments' (Humble, TX, 336 units) matched TDLR Project #TABS2025003997 (New Construction, completion 9/30/2026, owner RK Commercial Properties). Data is real and traceable.

CONFUSING:
- 'Early-stage construction signals' — I wasn't instantly sure whether it was news, permits, or something else until I read the sample table.
- 'Signals' vs 'leads' — the site used both; took a moment to realize a 'signal' becomes a 'lead' you call on.
- 'Why call now' column — the wording assumed I'd know the sales motion; I had to infer it meant a timely reason to reach out.
- 'RAS' (Registered Accessibility Specialist) appeared on the government page — jargon I didn't recognize as relevant to me.
- The 'agent' mentioned as a free-account perk was vague — I wasn't sure if it was an AI assistant or a CRM feature.
- The sign-in panel appeared as an overlay/chat panel, so at first I wasn't sure if it was a support chat or the actual login wall.

MISSING:
- Clear upfront pricing (it's 'free during early access', but no future price or what happens after).
- A visible data-coverage map or list of metros — I only confirmed Texas (592 leads); unclear if other states exist.
- Data-freshness detail beyond 'Sep 15 2026' — no stated refresh cadence.
- Any customer proof / testimonials / case studies to trust it beyond the sample table.
- A simple 'here's exactly what you get free vs. paid' comparison — I had to piece it together.
- Contact details on the lead (name/phone) were locked, so I couldn't judge lead quality without signing up.

FIRST 5 SECONDS: I thought it was a lead-generation / sales-intelligence tool that tells apartment-building vendors which buildings are being built or renovated soon, so they know who to call and when. Basically 'construction signals for people who sell to multifamily properties.'

WOULD SIGN UP: Yes, probably — but with hesitation. The site clearly explained why it wants my email (to unlock the manager name/direct phone and export the call list), it's no-card free during early access, and I verified the data traces to real public records, so the value is credible. The only reason I'd pause is that pricing-after-early-access isn't stated, so I'd want to know the cost before relying on it.

SCORE: 8/10 — easy to understand and the data proved real, but the 'signals/leads/agent' terminology and the hidden future pricing cost a couple of points.
