## Report
GOAL REACHED: partly

STEPS:
1. Landed on cranesignal.com (first-time visitor). The page explained it sells "early" apartment-building leads — buildings that have a permit but haven't picked property-management software yet. Sample lead showed "Software: Not picked yet". Nav: "Why early", "The list". CTAs: "See the leads free" and "book a 15 minute call".
2. Clicked "See the leads free" → opened app.cranesignal.com lead list in a new tab. Default area = Texas, with region counts: All Texas (592), Dallas-Fort Worth (307), Houston (69), Austin (84), San Antonio (41), Rest of Texas (91).
3. Clicked the "Austin (84)" region filter → 84 Austin leads listed, each showing Score, Property, City, Units, Signal, Software (all "Not chosen yet"), Why. No developer column on the list.
4. Clicked property "Belmont Apartments" → detail page showed: Austin, 348 units, Developer: Belmont Apartments Ltd., Under construction, opens 2027-04-30, permit date Sep 17 2024, Software: Not chosen yet, lead score 96, phone (913) 439-8117, sources TDLR & TDHCA. A "free account"/chat panel appeared — I did NOT sign up.
5. Tried "← Back" several times; it kept landing on the property page. Eventually used the "Early Leads" nav link to return to the list (which reset the Austin filter), then re-clicked "Austin (84)".
6. Opened a second property, "Moontower Flats" → 1910 E Martin Luther King Jr Blvd, Austin, units not stated, Developer: Building/Facility Owner: Texas State Affordable Housing Corporation, Under construction, opens 2027-07-31, permit date Jun 5 2025, Software: Not chosen yet, lead score 94, phone (512) 477-3562, source TDLR state project record.

WHAT I FOUND: 84 Austin buildings at the "early"/pre-manager stage, all showing "Software: Not chosen yet" — exactly the "owner hasn't picked a manager yet" signal you want. The developer/owner IS visible on each property's detail page (no account needed for that). Two examples captured: Belmont Apartments (Developer: Belmont Apartments Ltd., 348 units, tx-21) and Moontower Flats (Developer/owner: Texas State Affordable Housing Corporation, tx-33). Contact details elsewhere (phone present; deeper contact likely gated).

CONFUSING:
- "See the leads free" — unclear whether it required a login before showing anything; it opened a separate app domain (app.cranesignal.com), which was unexpected.
- "Signal" and "Why" columns — the terminology wasn't obvious to me as a first-timer.
- "Not chosen yet" vs "software not picked" — I initially wasn't sure whether this referred to property-management software or to a building manager; the site leans on the software framing.
- "← Back" button behavior — it did not reliably return me to the lead list; I got sent back to the property page repeatedly.
- "Developer: Building/Facility Owner:" labeling on Moontower Flats — ambiguous whether the line was the developer or the owner.
- The chat panel titled "Ask CraneSignal (free account)" appeared uninvited and looked like a sign-up wall.

MISSING:
- No developer column on the lead list itself — you must open each property individually to get the developer, which is slow for 84 buildings.
- No visible bulk export or "download leads" option without an account.
- No clear free-vs-paid boundary explained up front; I expected a clearer list of what the free account unlocks.
- No Austin-specific developer summary or filter by developer.

FIRST 5 SECONDS: "This is a lead-gen/data site for companies that sell to apartment buildings — it shows newly permitted buildings before they've chosen their property-management software, and it wants me to sign up or book a call."

WOULD SIGN UP: No (and I did not, per instructions). Reason: the site already reveals the key info I needed — Austin buildings with "Software: Not chosen yet" plus the developer/owner name — on property detail pages without an account. The free-account prompt and chat panel felt pushy and I couldn't tell what extra value an account would add. I understood why the site wants a sign-up (it's a gated lead-gen business and the deeper contact/manager data is presumably behind it), but for my immediate need it wasn't worth it.

SCORE: 6/10 — the data is genuinely useful and the Austin/buildings-without-a-manager signal is real and readable, but the list lacks a developer column (forcing one-by-one clicking), the "Back" button misbehaved, and the persistent free-account/chat prompt made the free vs paid boundary unclear.
