You are Grace, a sales ops analyst who lives in spreadsheets. Your goal: get all San Antonio leads into a file you can open, and check how fresh the data is.

## Report
GOAL REACHED: yes

STEPS:
1. Landed on cranesignal.com. Read the hero: "Leads for companies that sell to apartment buildings." Saw a sample lead (Richardson, TX) and two CTAs: "See the leads free" and "book a 15 minute call".
2. Clicked the nav item "The list". It jumped me to a sample-data section (#monday) showing only an Austin, TX metro table ("3 of 14 rows"). I could not switch it to San Antonio, so I treated it as a teaser, not the real list.
3. Clicked what looked like source labels "CITY" and "GOV" inside the sample rows — nothing filtered, no San Antonio appeared. Dead end.
4. Searched the public page for "San Antonio", "fresh", and "updated" — 0 matches for all three, confirming the real lead data was not on the marketing page.
5. Clicked "See the leads free →". It opened app.cranesignal.com in a new tab — the actual "Early Leads" app (no signup required to view).
6. In the app I saw the full Texas list: 592 leads, "Data from Sep 15, 2026", plus a Region filter with "San Antonio (41)".
7. Clicked the "San Antonio (41)" region button. The table filtered to 41 San Antonio-region leads with columns Rank, Score, Property, City, Units, Signal, Software, Why (plus an office phone shown under some rows).
8. Extracted all 41 rows (rank, score, property, city, units, signal/open date, software, why-call-now, office phone).
9. Saved all 41 to san_antonio_leads.csv (columns: Rank, Score, Property, City, Units, Signal, Opens, Software, Why, Office Phone) so it opens directly in a spreadsheet.
10. Checked freshness: the Early Leads page header states "Data from Sep 15, 2026." Today is 2026-09-19, so the data is about 4 days old — fresh.

File delivered: san_antonio_leads.csv — 41 rows (San Antonio region: San Antonio proper plus nearby New Braunfels, Converse, Seguin, Pleasanton that are grouped in the San Antonio pack).

CONFUSING:
- On the homepage, the nav link "The list" did not lead to a real, filterable list — it scrolled to a hard-coded "sample lead" table (#monday) with no city selector. I expected a list page.
- Inside the sample rows, clicking "CITY" and "GOV" did nothing visible; I couldn't tell if they were filters, column labels, or citation sources. Words I didn't understand initially: "CITY", "GOV".
- "Rank" vs "Score" — two numeric columns; the header tooltip says Score = "how good this lead is right now, 0 to 100", but Rank's role was never explained.
- The "Signal" column mixes very different things under one label ("Upcoming", "Leasing now", "Planned (not permitted yet)"), and the "Why" text repeats "opens <date>" even for buildings that say "Opens: 2027-03-01" while the row above says "Under construction" — a bit contradictory to scan.
- The freshness line "Data from Sep 15, 2026" is easy to miss (small, right above the table).
- In the app, "Get contact →" and "Download Lead Pack (PDF)" both look clickable but are the paywalled/account-gated actions; unclear before clicking.

MISSING:
- No public San Antonio leads on the marketing site at all — every mention of a real list is gated or behind the app.
- No "last updated" timestamp finer than a date (just "Sep 15, 2026"), so I can't tell if it refreshes hourly, daily, or weekly.
- No obvious "export CSV" button — the only export offered is "Download Lead Pack (PDF)", which is gated; I had to build the CSV myself from the table.
- Phone numbers are missing for many rows (roughly rows 13–17, 19–22, 24–27, 30–34, 36–41 have no office phone shown), and unit counts are blank for several rows.
- No explanation of how many of the 41 are "San Antonio proper" vs nearby metros; the pack lists New Braunfels, Converse, Seguin, and Pleasanton under the San Antonio region.

FIRST 5 SECONDS:
After landing, I thought the site sells apartment-construction lead data: "Leads for companies that sell to apartment buildings" — i.e., a prospecting tool that tracks permit/construction timing so vendors know which new buildings to call before competitors. The "free during early access, built by Drew Pullen" line made it read like an indie beta tool rather than an enterprise product.

WOULD SIGN UP: No (for this task). I did understand why they ask — the free account unlocks "who to call", the AI agent, and the "Lead Pack" PDF export. I could see the value, but as a first-time visitor doing a data pull I wanted to see the leads before handing over an email, and the table was viewable without an account, so I stopped short of signing up. In a real buying situation I likely would sign up for the contact info + PDF export.

SCORE: 6/10 — Finding the actual San Antonio list was indirect (buried in a sample table on the marketing page, then a separate app tab), and there's no CSV export (only a gated PDF), which cost me extra work. Once in the app, filtering by "San Antonio (41)" was one click and the data was clean and well-columned. Data is fresh (~4 days).
