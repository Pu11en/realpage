STEPS:
1. Landed on cranesignal.com. Header: "Leads for companies that sell to apartment buildings." Saw a sample lead in Richardson, TX with a green/red signal and buttons "See the leads free" and "or book a 15 minute call."
2. Clicked "See the leads free →" (opened a new tab at app.cranesignal.com).
3. Landed on the "Early Leads" list. Saw a region filter bar of Texas cities, including "Houston (69)".
4. Clicked "Houston (69)". URL changed to ?region=Houston and only Houston-region rows showed.
5. Read the table. Columns visible without an account: rank, building name, city, units, status/signal, timing, software chosen, and a notes column (permit/construction source links like "State permit data", "Houston weekly permit list").
6. Noticed a signup wall message: "A free account unlocks who to call, the agent and your Lead Pack." Every row had a "Get contact →" button behind that wall.
7. Scrolled to the bottom and clicked "Show more (50 of 69)" to reveal the rest.
8. Collected all 69 Houston-region leads (name, city, units, status, timing, software). 66 are Houston proper; the rest are metro-area cities (Sealy, Cleveland, etc.). All rows show software = "Not chosen yet". Saved to houston_leads.md.
9. Did NOT sign up. Noted the account requirement.

CONFUSING:
- The sample lead on the homepage was in Richardson, TX, not Houston, so I wasn't sure at first whether the site covered Houston. (word: "Richardson")
- Some rows had a blank "Units" — I didn't understand whether the data was unknown or hidden behind the account.
- Many building names are raw permit descriptions, not real names, e.g. "119,873 Sf New Apt Bld 1-5-3-R2/A3-A-Spk/Fa '21Ibc" — confusing to read.
- The same note tag "State permit data" sometimes appeared twice on one row — didn't understand why.
- "Show more (50 of 69)" — hesitated because I wasn't sure if it would load more rows or bounce me to a signup page.
- One row showed a phone with a 210 area code for a Houston-area building — odd.

MISSING:
- Contact details: manager name, direct phone, and the "agent" — all hidden behind the free account. This is exactly the info needed to hand the team a usable lead list.
- A way to export/download without signing up. "Download Lead Pack PDF" and "Download CSV" buttons exist but appear tied to the account.
- Unit counts for several buildings.
- A simple "search by city" box — I had to scan a long filter bar to find Houston.

FIRST 5 SECONDS:
I thought it was a B2B lead-generation / sales-prospecting tool that tells companies selling to apartment buildings which new buildings are coming and who to contact there.

WOULD SIGN UP:
No — not on the spot. I understood the pitch (contact info + the person to call + a Lead Pack is behind a free account), and it's a reasonable trade. But as a busy first-time visitor on my phone I don't want to create an account from an unknown site just to view a list; I'd rather have my team evaluate it or see a sample of the actual contact data first so I know it's real before handing over my email.

SCORE: 6/10
