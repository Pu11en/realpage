# Persona 1

You are Maria, a sales rep at a company selling property-management software. Your goal: find one apartment building in the Dallas area that is about to pick software, and find out who you should call there.

## Pages visited
https://cranesignal.com
https://cranesignal.com/
https://app.cranesignal.com/index.html
https://app.cranesignal.com/index.html?region=Dallas%E2%80%93Fort+Worth

## Report
GOAL REACHED: partly

STEPS:
1. Landed on cranesignal.com. Hero/pitch: a tool that lists apartment buildings "worth a call now" for people selling to them.
2. Read the landing page. It explains you get "every lead" free, but who to call needs a free account. A sample lead was shown: "Legacy Arapaho," 250 E Arapaho Rd, Richardson, TX, 443 units, developer Legacy Partners, software "Not picked yet."
3. Clicked the "See the leads free" link — it opened a new tab at app.cranesignal.com showing a big table of Texas leads.
4. Saw region filters and clicked "Dallas–Fort Worth (307)" — the list narrowed to 307 DFW leads, all showing reason, units, city and a "Get contact →" button.
5. Identified several Dallas buildings whose software is "Not chosen yet": Bowser Multifamily (Dallas, opens 2027-04-05, 28 units, score 97, office (214) 232-9984), Inwood Senior Living (Dallas, score 96, opens 2027-05-31, office (214) 370-2632), Mockingbird Station East Phase 2 (Dallas, 395 units, score 95, opens 2027-07-23).
6. Clicked "Get contact →" on Bowser Multifamily. Instead of showing a name, the site opened a sign-up/sign-in wall saying a free account unlocks "who to call." I did NOT sign up.
7. Closed the panel and wrote this report.

CONFUSING:
- "CraneSignal" — couldn't tell from the name alone what it was (sounded like a drone/signal product, not a sales-lead tool).
- "Every lead," "who to call," "Lead Pack" and "why-now brief" — these terms only made sense after scrolling; initially I wasn't sure if "lead" meant an apartment lead or a sales lead.
- "Signal" column ("New builds / Opening soon / Recently sold") — took a moment to realize this meant *why the building is worth calling now*.
- "Not chosen yet" vs "Not picked yet" vs "Not on any software yet" — same idea worded three different ways, made me pause.
- "Score 97/100" — not obvious what the score is measuring at first glance.
- "Get contact →" — I wasn't sure whether it would show info or hit a wall until I clicked it.

MISSING:
- I expected the person's NAME and direct phone right there since the sample lead teased "who to call" — instead it's locked behind an account.
- No prices/plans for a paid tier were visible (only "free during early access, no card"), so I couldn't judge long-term cost.
- No way to see even ONE full example contact without signing up.
- No clear "talk to sales / contact us" path for someone just window-shopping.

FIRST 5 SECONDS:
I thought it was some kind of monitoring/signal service (the name "CraneSignal" and "Early Leads" gave no clue). It only became clear after reading that it's a B2B lead-generation tool that tells software sellers which apartment buildings are about to buy software.

WOULD SIGN UP: No. Two reasons: (1) I was explicitly told as a first-time visitor not to create an account; and (2) I could not see the value — the person's name/phone is the ONLY thing behind the wall, and I can't verify it's accurate without trusting them, so signing up (even "free") felt like giving contact data for an unproven payoff. I understood WHY they gate it (more accounts = more sales), so the model is logical, I just wouldn't convert cold.

SCORE: 6/10 — Easy to read and browse leads without logging in, filtering by city works well, and the sample lead instantly showed me a relevant Dallas building. It loses points because the name "CraneSignal" is unclear, the terminology is repetitive, and the single most valuable thing (who to call) is walled off with no preview, which made me hesitate rather than convert.
