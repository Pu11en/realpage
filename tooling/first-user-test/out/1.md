# Persona 1

You are Maria, a sales rep at a company selling property-management software. Your goal: find one apartment building in the Dallas area that is about to pick software, and find out who you should call there.

https://cranesignal.com
https://cranesignal.com/

## Report
GOAL REACHED: partly

(Found a Dallas-area building about to pick software — Bowser Multifamily — and identified who to call, but the contact info required for CraneSignal itself was gated behind a free account sign-up, which I was told not to create. The call-list contact I found came from the building's public state project record, not from CraneSignal.)

STEPS:
1. Landed on cranesignal.com. Saw a sample lead card: "Legacy Arapaho", 250 E Arapaho Rd, Richardson TX, 443 units, expected opening ~Nov 2028, developer Legacy Partners, software "Not picked yet".
2. Scrolled the homepage. It sells lists of new apartment buildings before they choose property-management software. "Free early access", "one metro free", advertised "who to call: manager, phone, website".
3. Clicked "Open CraneSignal" — opened a new tab at app.cranesignal.com with a map and an "Early Leads" section.
4. Clicked "Early Leads" — a leads table loaded with filters (Texas/Arizona; regions incl. "Dallas–Fort Worth (307)").
5. Clicked the "Dallas–Fort Worth (307)" region filter — narrowed to 307 DFW leads, each with a "Get contact →" button.
6. Clicked "Get contact →" on a Dallas building — it opened an Ask CraneSignal chat panel with a sign-in/sign-up form (email/password, Google, sign up). Contact info is gated.
7. Tried "Download Lead Pack (PDF)" — also gated: "Sign in in the account panel to start your download."
8. Scrolled the DFW table to see Dallas-area rows. Best candidate: Bowser Multifamily, Dallas, 28 units, opens 2027-04-05, software "Not chosen yet".
9. Clicked the row's "State project record" link — opened a public Texas TDLR (Architectural Barriers) page in a new tab.
10. Read the TDLR record for Bowser Multifamily, 4516 Bowser Avenue, Dallas, TX 75219: New Construction, 28-unit, 3-story multifamily; Completion 4/5/2027 (matches CraneSignal's open date); Estimated cost $3,327,450. WHO TO CALL: Owner = Siekielski Matt & Shea Living Trust; Owner contact name "Gerard Whelan"; Owner phone 214-232-9984. (Also listed: RAS contact William Ledbetter / Christopher John Jacobs, (972) 707-3530; Design Firm Midtown Architects, (214) 396-5573 — these are not the software decision-makers.)

CONFUSING:
- "RAS", "RAS Name", "RAS #" on the state record — didn't know what RAS meant until the footnote explained it's a "Registered accessibility specialist" (not relevant to software).
- The homepage said contact info is "free", yet in the app the "Get contact →" button just opened a sign-in chat — unclear whether it was broken or gated.
- "View as" dropdown in the app — no idea what personas could be viewed or why.
- Difference between "Early Leads" and other lead categories wasn't explained.
- "Download Lead Pack (PDF)" implied a download but silently required login.

MISSING:
- Any actual contact/"who to call" info on CraneSignal without creating an account — everything was behind the sign-up wall.
- Pricing for the paid product once past "free early access" / "one metro free".
- A clear statement up front that contacts require an account.
- Explanation of what "View as" and the lead filters do.

FIRST 5 SECONDS: I thought the site was a lead-generation / database tool that lists brand-new apartment buildings before they've chosen their property-management software, so software vendors can pitch them early — i.e., a prospecting tool for property-software salespeople.

WOULD SIGN UP: No. The offer is genuinely relevant to what I do (new Dallas buildings before they pick software, with a promised phone number), so free early access is tempting — but the value is opaque until after you sign up, there's no pricing shown, and the one thing I actually wanted (the contact) stayed hidden. I understood WHY it required an account (they're protecting the leads that are their product), but I wouldn't hand over my email without at least seeing one contact card or a price first.

SCORE: 5/10. Clear niche and a compelling pitch on the homepage, and the app browsed well without login; but the promised free contact info was silently gated, and I had to leave the site and use a public state record to actually get the decision-maker's name and phone.
