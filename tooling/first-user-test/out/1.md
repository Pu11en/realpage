CONFUSING: (a) The very first thing I saw on the home page was a single lead card "LEAD #1 OF 10" — I hesitated because I wasn't sure if this was the whole product or just a teaser of a list. (b) "No software yet" — clear enough, but I briefly wondered whether it meant the building truly has no property-management system or just no record. (c) The phrase "Covered now: Texas, Arizona. (d) "Free during early access, no card" made me pause — I couldn't tell if this was genuinely free or a trial that later charges. (e) After clicking "See who to call →" a sign-in/chat panel appeared on top of the table and I was briefly unsure whether the phone number was the gated contact or the free one. (f) "Ask the agent" / "Ask CraneSignal" — I wasn't sure what it did versus just searching the list.

MISSING: The actual point of contact — a name and a direct line — was the main thing missing. It was promised ("a free account unlocks: manager name, direct phone") but never shown without signup. I also expected, and did not find, any plain per-building detail page; the "Why" cell is just a one-line summary. There was no way to see contact info or download the call list/spreadsheet without an account.

FIRST 5 SECONDS: After landing, I thought cranesignal.com was some kind of construction/permit data site or a marketing page about "apartment signals" — the name plus the "LEAD #1 OF 10" card made it read like an investor/prospecting list rather than a tool for sellers. It took reading the subhead about buildings "being built, opening soon, or just sold" before I realized it's a sales-prospecting lead list for vendors who sell to apartment buildings — basically what I'd want.

WOULD SIGN UP: No. Two reasons. First, the instructions for this visit were explicit not to sign up (no account). Second, even setting that aside, I would have hesitated: the site never showed me a full sample of what the gated "manager name + direct phone" actually looks like — I was asked to trade an email for a promise. It does say "no card" and "free during early access," which lowers the risk, and if I were doing this for real work for a few dollars' worth of time I probably would sign up, because the lead quality (a Dallas building with "No software yet" opening 2027) is exactly my target. So: not on this visit, but likely yes in real life.

SCORE: 6/10. Easy to read and the leads are genuinely relevant and well-filtered (region filter, "no software yet" flag, opening dates). It lost points because (1) the single most valuable thing — the contact person — is hidden behind signup with no preview, (2) a chat/sign-in panel popped over the table mid-task, and (3) there's no per-building detail page. Functionally it did its job; friction is mostly the paywall-style gate.
