# Ten number-first CraneSignal video ideas

Draft narration for you holding your phone and showing your computer — read these aloud and keep whichever sounds like you.

The counts come from today's launch brief; match them to the screen before filming. A correction to the earlier ideas: a sale or permit does **not** prove a building has no software, is changing vendors, or has never been contacted.

## 1 · Big money, specific building

### A — “I'd want to know”

“22.6 million dollars. That's what this apartment building sold for in August. If I sold software to apartment owners, I'd want to know who just bought it. This is CraneSignal. It flags buildings that sold so you've got somewhere specific to start looking.”

### B — “Wrong reason to look”

“22.6 million dollars. I'm looking at this apartment sale, but I'm not trying to buy it. I'm thinking: if you sell property management software, would you want the new owner on your prospect list? That's the kind of building you can find here.”

## 2 · A list that needs narrowing

### C — “Which would you call?” ⭐

“1,876 apartment buildings. If you sell software to apartment owners, which one would you call first? I'd start with what changed. CraneSignal tracks permits and sales, so I can look for a reason to call before I start writing a pitch. You can browse it free.”

### D — “Start with the 175”

“175 apartment buildings with a permit or sale in the last six months. That's the part of this list I'd look at first if I sold apartment software. Open a building, check what happened, then decide whether it's worth a conversation. That's what I'm showing you here.”

## 3 · Talk like a seller

### E — “Give me a reason”

“One reason to call. That's what I'd want before pitching software to an apartment owner. Did they buy a building? Is something being planned? CraneSignal puts those buildings in a list. I'd rather open with something happening at their property than another ‘just checking in.’”

### F — “What would I actually say?”

“Two ways to start a sales email. ‘Wanted to introduce myself.’ Or, ‘Saw you bought this apartment building — are you reviewing the software there?’ If I sold apartment software, I'd try the second. CraneSignal helps me find the sale to start that research.”

## 4 · Curiosity and a reveal

### G — “A building before the residents”

“938 apartment buildings being built or planned, all on this list. If you sell software to apartment owners, would you look here for prospects? I'd check the project first, then research who's behind it. CraneSignal gives you the starting list, and browsing is free.”

### H — “The second question”

“256 apartments. One building. It sold in August. If I wanted to pitch software here, finding the building is only the first question. The second is: who should I actually talk to? CraneSignal has an AI research tool for that — you unlock it with a free account.”

## 5 · Direct offer or invite a response

### I — “Just show me the list”

“1,876 apartment buildings you can look through without making an account. This is CraneSignal, for people selling software and services to apartment owners. It tracks buildings being planned, built, or sold. Browse free; make a free account for contact research and downloads.”

### J — “Make the viewer choose”

“Two apartment buildings. One is being planned. One just sold. If you sell property management software, which would you research first? CraneSignal tracks both. I'd check the sale first to learn who's taking over — tell me which one you'd pick and why.”

**For any version that needs a closing line:** “It's CraneSignal — free early access, and I'd like your feedback.”

**If you show contact research or exports:** Say “You'll need a free account for this part”; browsing needs no login.

These are ideas to choose from, not customer testimonials or claims that you have used them to close sales.
