# How Neighborhoods Shape Multifamily Performance

The submarket is one of the most relied-upon benchmarks in multifamily, but it’s also one of the most misleading. Submarket boundaries are drawn around zip codes and roads, not around the way renters actually choose where to live. Two properties a few blocks apart can perform like they’re in completely different markets, and the blended average tells you nothing meaningful about either one.
In this research, RealPage introduces demand pockets as an analytical lens for identifying where a single anchor creates a measurable performance advantage that the surrounding submarket average cannot see. Using Dallas as a case study, the analysis examines how pockets form, what drives their durability, and what changes when an anchor disappears.
Whether you’re acquiring assets, managing a portfolio through a soft market, or pressure-testing a renovation thesis, this session gives you a sharper tool for one of the most consequential location decisions in multifamily.
You will gain insight into:
  * What a demand pocket is and how it differs from a submarket boundary
  * How to identify the anchor driving a pocket and what anchor type tells you about durability
  * Which Dallas pockets showed the strongest rent performance and which ones did not
  * How pocket-level results compared to the submarket average through past market corrections
  * What the demand pocket framework means for site selection, underwriting, repositioning, and hold decisions
