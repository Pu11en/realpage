## 🏢 Is calling newly sold buildings a good idea? Mostly yes, with one catch
- ✅ **A sale is one of the best times to reach them.** New owners often bring in a new management company, and that company brings its own software.
- ⚠️ **The catch: the building usually doesn't choose the software. The management company's head office does** (here, Bell Partners or Asset Living). The (844) number is almost surely a leasing call center.
- So the real call is to **the management company's regional or head office**, and the building is the reason you're calling. The AI already worked this out in its objection answer.
- The buildings still under construction (like Legacy Arapaho, 443 units) work the same way: find the developer, call them.

## ⚖️ Is it legal to cold call? (plain facts, not legal advice)
- ✅ **Calling businesses by hand is generally legal in the US.** The Do Not Call list is mainly for people's home and cell numbers, not business lines.
- ⚠️ **What gets people in trouble:** auto-dialers, recorded messages, and texts to cell phones without permission. Dial by hand and you avoid most of it.
- ⚠️ **Texas has its own telemarketing rules.** Selling to businesses is often exempt, but check before running a real calling campaign.
- 🚫 **The real problem: you don't work for RealPage.** Calling buildings and saying "I'm with RealPage" is misrepresentation. It could get you in legal trouble, and it would ruin the pitch if RealPage heard about it.

## 🎯 What that means for you
- **Your customer is RealPage, not the apartment buildings.** The thing to show them is "here are 42 buildings to call this week, and here's a ready call sheet for each one."
- **If you want real calls anyway, call as yourself.** For example: "I'm building a tool about apartment software. Who picks the software at your company, and when do you review it?" That's honest, legal, and gives you real quotes to show RealPage.
- **Or call RealPage directly** and show them the tool live. That's the call that makes you money.
